METHODOLOGY

Model assumptions

Election schedule and starting composition.

Start with 56 Democratic caucus seats, including two independents, and 44 Republican seats after the 2026 election. Carry forward seats that are not on the ballot. Class III votes in 2028, Class I in 2030, and Class II in 2032. Ohio and Florida’s 2026 special elections filled seats contested again in 2028.

Each state has two fixed baselines.

For both 2024 and 2026, use that year’s Senate race when available; otherwise sum Democratic and Republican House votes across the state. Each future performance setting is relative to those fixed baselines, regardless of earlier selections.

Two ways to move the margins.

Uniform: 2026 state margin + chosen point shift. The “2024 (approx)” waterline at −14 points is only a rough national reference.
Interpolation: 2024 margin + chosen percentage × (2026 margin − 2024 margin). 0% matches each state’s 2024 baseline; 100% matches 2026. −50% and 150% extend that state’s trend beyond those years.

Seat assignment and model limits.

A positive D-minus-R margin assigns a seat to the Democratic side; a negative margin assigns it to the Republican side. Exact ties stay unresolved. There is no probability model, candidate effect, turnout forecast, or assumption about a future vice president. A 50–50 Senate is labeled a tie.

Alaska scenario assumption

The model fixes Lisa Murkowski’s Class III seat as Republican in 2028 at every setting. This is an explicit scenario assumption, not a candidate forecast. Alaska’s separate Class II seat is carried forward as Democratic through 2030 and modeled from vote margins in 2032.

Blackburn’s election as governor is expected to put her Class I seat on the 2028 ballot. The seat is then contested again in 2030. Other unannounced vacancies and special elections are omitted.

Data sources, margin definitions & independent candidates

Snapshot: November 18, 2026. 2026 counts are provisional and are frozen in this app. Sources can have different update times; this is not a live results feed.

2026 Senate margins and House candidate vote totals: Call the Map, compiled from state and county election offices. 2024 results: U.S. House Clerk’s official election statistics. Election classes: U.S. Senate. Starting caucus: Ballotpedia. Tennessee: AP’s reporting on Blackburn’s succession.

Senate margins are the leading Democratic-side candidate’s share minus the leading Republican’s share of votes cast, including other candidates in the denominator. In 2024, regular full-term races take precedence over special elections. House fallback margins use (total D votes − total R votes) ÷ (total D votes + total R votes) × 100. This sums votes, rather than averaging district margins. Uncontested and same-party House races can distort the fallback.

Where an independent is the main opposition to a Republican, their margin is used as a Democratic-side proxy: King and Sanders in 2024; Osborn in 2024 and 2026; Bodnar, Achilles, and Bengs in 2026. This is a modeling convention, not a claim that these candidates would caucus with Democrats. In Montana in 2026, Bodnar’s margin is used instead of the lower-polling Democratic candidate. Ranked-choice and multi-candidate races add further simplifications.
